HMOS as a hospitality theory

Proposed definition. The Hospitality Management Operating System (HMOS) is a role-based, evidence-gated management architecture that translates enterprise strategy into operational conditions, measures results, tests managerial control, coordinates cross-department dependencies, closes corrective actions and converts verified learning into institutional memory.
IDTheoretical statementObservable test
P1 AlignmentWhen strategic objectives are translated into role-specific control conditions, frontline activity should become more visibly connected to enterprise strategy.Trace enterprise objective → role → process/shift → evidence.
P2 EvidenceWhen performance claims require identifiable evidence, unsupported or ambiguous scores should decline.Measure % of KPI records with complete evidence.
P3 ControlWhen managerial evaluation distinguishes result from managerial control, accountability should become more precise.Compare outcome-only appraisal with control-and-evidence appraisal.
P4 CoordinationWhen cross-department dependencies are explicit KPIs, unresolved bottlenecks should become more visible.Track dependency closure time, repeat failures and escalations.
P5 InstitutionalizationWhen corrective action must become a documented control, repeat failures should decline.Measure recurrence after CAPA closure and re-audit.
P6 LearningWhen evidence flows through a recurring rhythm, reusable management knowledge should accumulate.Measure reuse of SOPs, audit findings, training and improvement records.

Implementation sequence

StepAction
01 Define the enterprise standardBrand promise, strategic objectives, financial boundaries, compliance and service standards.
02 Define positionsUse positions rather than individuals as the durable units of accountability.
03 Build the KPI dictionaryFor each KPI define target, direction, measurement method, evidence, frequency, owner and control test.
04 Connect daily workMap SOPs, shifts, checklists, handovers and system transactions to the relevant KPI.
05 Establish the evidence gateNo identifiable evidence → incomplete record; do not silently convert missing evidence into a score.
06 Run the management rhythmDaily evidence; weekly exception review; monthly evidence register; quarterly formal evaluation; annual appraisal.
07 Close the loopRecord variance, corrective action, verification and institutionalization.
08 Build the data layerIntegrate operational systems only after management definitions are stable.
09 Add analytics and AIIntroduce predictive and assistive functions after evidence quality and governance are reliable. AI assists; managers authorize.
